▶📅Measuring every day
Measurement is the basis of science and part of daily life: time (when school starts), length (how far, how tall), mass (vegetables, sugar, rice), temperature (when we are ill) and volume (milk, oil).
▶✋Measuring with our bodies
Long ago, length was measured with the body: arm length or cubit (elbow to the tip of the middle finger), foot length, a footstep, a pace, a handspan, the width of four fingers and a yard length (nose to fingertips, arm stretched out).
▶👣Why body units fail
Five friends measuring one room with their feet get five readings, because feet differ. Body parts differ from person to person, so body units are unreliable; standard units keep measurements uniform.
▶⚖️Measuring is comparing
Measurement is the comparison of an unknown quantity with a known constant quantity, or unit. A unit is the fixed quantity we compare with: a metre for length, a kilogram for mass, a second for time.
▶🔢A number and a unit
Every measurement has a number and the name of a unit, and is not complete without both. 1.5 m is 1.5 times the standard length of 1 m.
▶📏Physical quantities
Any quantity that can be measured is a physical quantity. Physical quantity = numerical value × unit. Physics is called the science of measurement.
▶👌A good standard (i–ii)
A standard unit has a moderate size, so it is convenient, and space and time do not affect its value: it is the same everywhere and always.
▶🛡️A good standard (iii–v)
It is defined without any doubt, it is not perishable (it cannot be destroyed), and it is easily reproducible (exact copies can be made).
▶🇫🇷The metric system
First adopted in France in 1791. MKS: metre, kilogram, second. CGS, for smaller quantities: centimetre, gram, second.
▶🌍The SI system
Recommended in 1960 for the whole world. Seven basic units: metre, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole, candela. Length, mass, time and temperature are basic quantities.
▶🏛️India’s standards
India uses the MKS system. The National Physical Laboratory, New Delhi, keeps the national standards; the metre rods and kilograms in our markets are copies.
▶📏Measuring length
Length is the distance between two points; height, depth, thickness and perimeter are all lengths. SI unit: the metre (once two marks on a bar in Paris); CGS unit: the centimetre. Tools: ruler, metre scale, tape; Vernier callipers and screw gauge for small lengths.
▶🪜The length ladder
km = 1000 m · hm = 100 m · dam = 10 m · dm = 1/10 m · cm = 1/100 m · mm = 1/1000 m. 1 inch = 2.54 cm; 12 inches = 1 foot. A light year is a distance; micron = 10⁻⁶ m; angstrom = 10⁻¹⁰ m.
▶👁️Measuring correctly
Scale along the length, zero at one edge; eye vertically above (no parallax error); worn end: start at a full mark and subtract. Estimate to the nearest mm (or cm for metres). Tiny lengths: measure many, then divide; curves: a thread.
▶🟩Measuring area
Area is the surface enclosed by a boundary; SI unit m². Square a², rectangle l × b, triangle ½ bh, circle π r². Irregular shapes: count squares on graph paper. 1 m² = 10000 cm²; are = 100 m²; hectare = 10000 m²; km² for a country.
▶🪨Measuring mass
Mass is the amount of matter (kg; CGS: g). Tonne = 10 quintals = 1000 kg; quintal = 100 kg; 1 g = 1000 mg. Weight is the pull of gravity and changes from place to place; mass does not. In 2019 the kilogram got a definition that does not change.
▶⚖️Balances
Grocer’s and beam balances compare with standard masses; a true beam balance has equal pans, arms and strings. A physical balance measures milligrams (glass case, weight box, not for hot objects); an electronic balance shows mass to 0.01 g.
▶⏱️Measuring time
Time is the interval between two events; its SI unit is the second. People first watched the sun and the moon (year, month, day); then came the sundial, sand clock and water clock; today, clocks, watches, stopwatches and the atomic clock, the most accurate.
▶🪜The time ladder
60 s = 1 min · 60 min = 1 h (3600 s) · 24 h = 1 day · 365¼ days = 1 year. To a smaller unit, multiply; to a bigger unit, divide. The 24-hour clock: take away 1200 for afternoon and evening times.
▶✍️Writing units correctly
Names in small letters (Celsius and Fahrenheit keep capitals); symbols of scientists’ units in capitals (K, N, J, °C); other symbols small (m, kg, s, g); no full stop; no plural; a space after the number.

  • France, 1791: the metric system; MKS and CGS

  • NPL, New Delhi keeps India’s standards
